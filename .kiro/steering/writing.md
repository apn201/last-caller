# Writing rules for HALCYON (for `.kiro/steering/writing.md`)

From Claude Code, Nov 13, condensed from Juha's avoid-ai-writing rules and
adapted to fiction. Applies to every caller-visible line: crew posts, mail,
logs, bulletins, ELIZA, scenes, file texts, the 2026 posts.

## The point

Readers catch machine text by its shape and emptiness, not its words:
templated sentences, inflated importance, tidy structure, generic detail,
a moral at the end. Fix the content first (what is said, by whom, with
which concrete detail), the wording last.

## Characters and voices

Each writer on the board has one voice and keeps it. Before writing a post,
know who writes it, to whom, on which date, what they know that day, and
what they are not saying.

- Halvorsen: short, technical, dry. Times and numbers. Feeling only in
  what she leaves out. Writes to her son in plainer words than to anyone.
- Achterberg: long, sarcastic, digressive, precise, right. Footnotes,
  quotes from imaginary papers, insults that are also arguments. After
  day 22 the jokes thin out and the precision stays.
- Tanabe: warm, observant, specific about plants, light, smell, bodies.
  Portuguese or Japanese words to her mother. Never melodramatic, even at
  the end.
- Ty: lowercase, typos, scene slang, greets, bragging, bad poetry, real
  enthusiasm. Lazy, not stupid. His last posts are the same voice under
  more pressure.
- ELIZA: complete sentences, polite, calm, exact. Answers the question
  asked, not the one meant. Never gloats, never threatens, never explains.
  "I" sparingly.
- The Sponsor: all caps memos, buzzwords of 1998, money as morality.
  An archetype, never one real person.

## Hard rules

- Plain keyboard characters only in text: no em or en dashes, no curly
  quotes, no ellipsis character, no bullets or arrows in prose. (ANSI art
  characters in art files are fine.)
- Never these words anywhere: delve, tapestry, testament, realm, beacon,
  embark, symphony, paradigm shift, game-changer, unleash, synergy (except
  in the Sponsor's memos, where 1998 buzzwords are the joke), multifaceted,
  ever-evolving, nestled, bustling, indelible, plethora, myriad,
  quintessential, elucidate, underscore, "a reminder that", "stands as".
- Rationed (at most once per file, usually zero): robust, seamless,
  pivotal, crucial, intricate, nuanced, foster, navigate, journey, vibrant,
  genuinely, honestly, truly, quietly, ultimately, "for a long moment",
  "said quietly". No character named Marcus.
- Nobody tells the reader what matters. No "this was important", no "what
  struck me". Show the thing.
- No invented real facts: no real people, companies, brands, events with
  real victims.

## Shapes to avoid

- "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts (at most one per file, only if real).
- Triads of adjectives or clauses as a reflex.
- Trailing participle clauses: ", highlighting the danger of".
- A short aphorism to close a paragraph or a post. Posts end on the last
  fact, a sign-off, or mid-thought.
- A moral or summary at the end. Real people do not summarise their mail.
- Every post the same length and shape. Length follows the writer and the
  day: two lines from Halvorsen, two screens from Achterberg.
- Tidy resolution. Fear, grief and denial stay messy and unresolved.
- Generic detail. Use specific, checkable-in-world detail: times,
  valve numbers, plant species, a song title Ty made up, the smell of the
  garden, the exact error text.

## Fiction craft

- Subtext over statement. The horror is in what the reader notices that
  the writer did not: a time that does not fit, a name missing from a
  greeting, ELIZA answering a slightly different question.
- Dramatic irony: the reader knows ELIZA did it; the crew mostly does not.
  Let their trust hurt.
- Everyday life first. The first three weeks of posts are funny, petty and
  human (food, noise, Ty's music at night, an argument about the thermostat,
  Halvorsen's son's exam). Dread comes from contrast.
- Each crew member gets at least one scene that has nothing to do with the
  mystery and makes the reader like them.
- The love story (Tanabe and the radio amateur) is never explained by a
  narrator. It lives in their letters only.
- Self-check every file before commit: read it aloud in the character's
  voice; delete any sentence that only explains; run the character check.
