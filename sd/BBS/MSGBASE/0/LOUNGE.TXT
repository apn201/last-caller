Dr. Tanabe is correct and for the wrong reason, which is the only way anyone
has ever been correct about a thermostat.

I have logged the cabin sensor for thirty-one hours. It does not hold 21.0. It
swings between 20.7 and 21.3 with a period of forty-one minutes, because the
controller has a dead band of half a degree and the air handler has a lag of
about six minutes. Your hands are not a thermometer, but they have noticed a
control loop that hunts. [1]

The display shows the average. The average is 21.0 in the way a man with one
foot in the oven and one in the freezer is, on average, comfortable.

It is a small thing. I mention it because small things that are reported as
fine are my profession. The insurers pay me for it. They do not pay me enough
to be cold.

Prof. D. Achterberg, risk adviser

[1] Van der Meer and Okonkwo, "Hunting in Domestic Control Loops", J. Applied
    Frustration 4 (1987), pp. 112-131. Required reading. I wrote it.
