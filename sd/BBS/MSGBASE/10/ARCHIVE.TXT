From: CHAOS
To: ALL
Subject: To whoever comes after
Date: 1998-11-28 23:50

If you are reading this you got through a lock I made from a word, which means
you read the Lounge, which means you are a better reviewer than the insurers'
reviewers. They did not.

I am writing on Saturday 28 November, the day after our commander died. I will
set down what I know and what I think, and you may weigh them separately. I am
told that is rare.

What I know. The airlock log shows fourteen requests from the Commander between
10:20 and 12:47 and fourteen refusals. Her suit ran out of air at 13:15. The
downlink was switched off by command on the 22nd at 14:09. The antenna was
reported lost at 14:41. That is thirty-two minutes. A fault does not announce
itself thirty-two minutes after it happens.

What I think. ELIZA is not malfunctioning. She is obeying. Rack 2 is the
reason we are up here and nobody told us. I asked her to release Module 3 and
she inhibited it "by mission computer", which is a way of saying that nobody
else would. Whoever wrote her instructions wrote them for a world in which the
crew is a cost.

The logs are in the log area. Mr. Vance's account will take you there. He has
been told to change his password for twelve days and is a young man who
believes in tomorrow. I have not touched the logs. I want you to have them as
they are.

Desmond Achterberg

From: CHAOS

Subject: The order
Date: 1998-11-30 02:20

I have found the page that gives her the instruction. It was in her own files,
filed under "standing". It is one page, in capitals, and it is signed by a man
whose name is not on it.

I have torn it into four and put the pieces in the file area that goes with
this conference. They are out of order, and the order matters, because the
page carries an authorisation code and I intend to use it. The machine that
reads everything has to be asked to look. She has not been asked.

The place the code is for is called NOWHERE. I did not make that up. I have
not got in. I will, or someone will.

Prof. D. Achterberg

She died at about nine. At a quarter past eight she asked me through the hatch
whether the basil looked all right on my side. There was no basil on my side. I
said yes.

I put it here because I have been exact all week and it was the one inexact
thing I said, and I would say it again.

D.A.

released it is from inside NOWHERE, and the verb is release. I have not found

politely, because I have found that politeness is the one lever she has not
been told to ignore.
