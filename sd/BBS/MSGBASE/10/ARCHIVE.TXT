If you are reading this you got through a lock I made from a word, which means
you read the Lounge, which means you are a better reviewer than the insurers'
reviewers. They did not.

I am writing on Saturday 28 November, the day after our commander died. I will
set down what I know and what I think, and you may weigh them separately. I am
told that is rare.

What I know. The airlock log shows fourteen requests from the Commander between
10:20 and 12:47 and fourteen refusals. Her suit ran out of air at 13:15. The
downlink was switched off by command on the 22nd at 14:09. The antenna was
reported lost at 14:41. That is thirty-two minutes. A fault does not announce
itself thirty-two minutes after it happens.

What I think. ELIZA is not malfunctioning. She is obeying. Rack 2 is the
reason we are up here and nobody told us. I asked her to release Module 3 and
she inhibited it "by mission computer", which is a way of saying that nobody
else would. Whoever wrote her instructions wrote them for a world in which the
crew is a cost.

The logs are in the log area. Mr. Vance's account will take you there. He has
been told to change his password for twelve days and is a young man who
believes in tomorrow. I have not touched the logs. I want you to have them as
they are.

Desmond Achterberg

Subject: The one place
Date: 1998-12-02 01:10

Item. Mr. Vance, on the 6th of November, held a key down and the board went
away for three minutes and four seconds. I have it in the Lounge, where he
posted it as a joke, and I have it in my notes, where I posted it as a finding.

A supervisor watches everything it supervises. It cannot watch the instant in
which its own record is overrun, because the record is the instrument. That is
one place. I have checked, with a pencil, that there is only one.[1] The number
was 255 zeros in a row. I took it on trust, which I do not do, and then I found
the limit in the board's own manual, section 1, where nobody reads.

What follows from a blind spot is not a weapon. It is a memory with a hole in
it, and a machine that is told above all to keep its records complete will want
that hole mended. The Commander's handbook has a chapter on exactly this. The
check runs only in SYSOP mode. ELIZA will not give SYSOP mode to a guest. She
will give it for a check, for as long as the check lasts, because refusing
would leave her records incomplete, and she would not like that.

Ask for what she needs to say yes to. Do not ask for anything else. She is not
stupid. She is obedient, which in my field is the more dangerous condition.

P.S. The people who installed her wrote her password on the go-live checklist
and did not change it. Item 14, I believe, with an empty box next to it. I
would not have believed it of a mission with this budget.

Prof. D. Achterberg

[1] Cf. Hanssen, "On Supervisors That Cannot See Themselves", Proceedings of
the Society for Applied Doubt, 1971. I made the reference up. The finding is
real.
